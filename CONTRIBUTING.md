Thank you for contributing to Pool Party. This repository's current first-party
distribution policy is [Pool Party Source-Available License 1.0](LICENSE).
Read [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md) and [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)
before copying code or introducing a dependency.

The default applies to current and future Pool Party-authored contributions,
including work in progress. Previously granted MIT, AGPL and upstream rights
remain available for the portions they cover. An existing license grant is not
revoked by submission of a new patch.

## Ownership and permission for new contributions

You retain copyright in your contribution. A Git commit, employment label or
pull request is not evidence that Pool Party owns somebody else's copyright.
Earlier contributions are not automatically assigned or relicensed by this
policy.

Before a new contribution is accepted under the first-party policy, include
this explicit statement in the pull request:

> I have the right to submit this contribution. For the parts I own or am
> authorized to license, I grant Pool Party Labs a worldwide, perpetual,
> irrevocable, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use, reproduce, modify,
> make derivative works, publicly display, perform, distribute and sublicense
> the contribution under the repository's source-available license and under
> separate commercial or proprietary licenses. This grant does not transfer
> my copyright. I have identified all third-party or separately licensed
> material and any limitations on my authority.

This separate inbound grant gives Pool Party the permission needed to publish
and license accepted contributions. It does not give you ownership of the
existing code or permission to relicense other people's work.

If an employer or another rightsholder controls the contribution, obtain their
authorization and identify it to the maintainers. A signed contributor
agreement may be required. This statement does not replace a signed agreement
when one is required, and a missing grant must be resolved before acceptance.
Patent rights, contributor identity and the correct contracting legal entity
must be addressed in the signed agreement where applicable.

## Third-party and mixed material

Preserve upstream licenses, source attribution and copyright notices. Identify
copied, generated or adapted material, its source/version and its license in
the pull request. Do not replace a third-party SPDX identifier with Pool Party's
identifier. Importing or compiling a dependency does not make it Pool Party
property.

The root policy cannot add incompatible restrictions to AGPL/GPL, font or
other separately licensed material. An upstream-boundary contribution must
retain its applicable license; disclose that exception rather than claiming
the entire patch is exclusively first-party.

## Review and validation

Keep pull requests focused, link the relevant issue, explain the behavior or
document change, and include validation appropriate to the scope. Licensing-only
changes need notice/scope checks; they do not require a full runtime test run.

Study/contribution forks and local tests are permitted under the license.
Separate production deployments and commercial products containing restricted
material require express written authorization. Independent adapters can
interoperate with the official Pool Party platform as described in the license.

For vulnerabilities, follow the repository's security reporting instructions;
do not include undisclosed exploits or credentials in public issues.

## Requesting other rights

Open a non-sensitive licensing request in this repository or contact a
maintainer through the verified [PoolPartyLabs organization](https://github.com/PoolPartyLabs).
A request is not permission. Additional rights require an agreement signed by
an authorized Pool Party representative identifying the legal entity, covered
material, versions and permitted use.
