## Summary
All four earlier findings in my scope reproduce on the integrated system with real swaps (04 H-01 with one refinement). None is refuted, and the automatic-unwind attack (report 04 C-01) is worse than reported.
- One whole share is enough when Free Idle is 0, which is the state right after the manager allocates all Idle. A Standard claim works as well as an Instant one.
- One claim closes every position of every pool in the unwind order.
- A third party can do it around another holder's claim, if it controls transaction ordering.
- It still pays, for a 100,000 USDC position, in a pool as deep as Arbitrum's Uniswap V3 WETH/USDC 0.05% pool. The pools the adapter can actually list cost about 118 USDC (Arbitrum) and 182 USDG (Robinhood) to crush and restore.

No single fix option closes the family. Option (a) closes the three Share Price routes, the new fallback route included, and fits in CoreVaultLogic. Options (b) and (c) stop the deep unwind and fit in the Spoke Vault together, but each still leaks up to its tolerance. Options (a)-for-sizing and (d) do not fit in the Spoke Vault as it is.

New findings: Critical 0, High 0, Medium 0, Low 1, Info 3.

Details behind the rows, as the coordinator asked:

**Shapes and depth of loss (04 C-01).** Pushing the pool to 1/1,000 of its price makes every in-range position all WETH. It also makes its spot value tiny, so any claim whose shortfall exceeds that value closes the whole position. The vault then sells the WETH at the pushed price. At block 510407446, for ±10%:
- the vault sold 38.08 WETH for 101.53 USDC;
- the exit returned 38.08 WETH and 0 USDC, where the same liquidity holds 17.57 WETH and 52,380 USDC at the oracle composition.

Shape does not matter for this deep push. A push that leaves the position in range leaks only the sale discount:
- **at −5.2%, with liquidity left under the price:** 37.1% of the liquidity, worth 36,961 at the oracle composition, became 35,053 USDC. Alice lost 1,705.
- **at −2%:** the fund lost 1.55% of the exited value with liquidity left under the price (Alice −504), and 1.74% when the sale filled against the pool's own liquidity (Alice −585, block 510409306).

**Minimum stake.** Three conditions:
- The claim unwinds only when its `wanted` (the request, capped at the balance's value at the claim-time price) exceeds Free Idle, plus the request's own reserve for a Standard claim. See `CoreVault.sol:155-160`.
- The first position is reached only when the target (shortfall × 1.02) exceeds the hub vault's Unallocated USDC (`SpokeVault.sol:537`, `:829-830`).
- The position closes whole when its spot value is at most the shortfall still needed (`SpokeVault.sol:859`). The attacker picks the push depth: at 1/1,000,000 of the price, 38 WETH are worth 0.10 USDC. The whole downside of the listed pool is 36.8 WETH, and the round trip cost 212.

The stake must predate the manager's allocation, because a deposit adds to Free Idle one for one (`CoreVault.sol:81`). With Free Idle and hub Unallocated USDC at 0, one share (a 2 USDC deposit) closed the whole position; the claim itself was paid 0.60 gross (block 510407446). Nothing in the protocol keeps a minimum Free Idle.

**Standard or Instant.** A Standard request reserves `min(amount, share value, Free Idle)` at request time (`CoreVault.sol:115`). When Free Idle was 0 at request, it reserves 0, and after the 72 h term the claim unwinds exactly like an Instant one (`test_unwind_minimumStake_standardPayoutToo`, +99,496). A fully reserved Standard claim never unwinds; only the Share Price route then applies.

**Third party.** Only the requester can claim (DEC-065). The attacker does not need to be that requester if its push lands before the victim's claim and its restore after:
- Tested with same-block ordering assumed: the attacker holds one share, Bruno claims 29,700 without hints. The attacker gained 99,422; Alice lost 88,912 and Bruno 11,109.
- On Arbitrum a stranger cannot see a pending transaction: the sequencer orders first come, first served and there is no public mempool. So this needs privileged ordering. Not tested, because a fork has no sequencer.
- Bruno's own oracle-floored hint stops it; see Fix options.

**Spoke window.** The moved report is priced for mints until its age exceeds `maxReportAge`: 1,588 s after `report.timestamp`, then `StaleSpokeReport`. It is priced for payouts until a report with a higher sequence is accepted, with no age limit (Q57 reading); 2 h later the +4,291.50 was still in Share Assets.
- **Displacing it:** anyone can call `report()` on Robinhood (message fee 0) and deliver the VAA once guardians sign the finalized message. DEC-099 puts that at about 925 to 1,190 s after publication.
- **Pre-empting it:** a keeper cannot. The attacker's own report finalizes no later than any report published after it, so a keeper only shortens the window for later mints and claims.
- **Round trip and break-even:** 230.58 USDG was measured for the ±10% spoke position, which is 26.8% of Share Assets. The break-even claim is about 30,000. Other shapes follow the hub table's ratios: the position is inflated by 1.37% (±5%), 2.87% (±10%) and 25.5% (±50%) at the lower edge.

Residual risk of each option:
- **(a)** moves the valuation onto Chainlink, as the Unallocated WETH leg already is. Its error is the pool-to-oracle gap, not a manipulable number: 0.004 to 0.05% for the listed pools at the probe blocks. The unwind still sells at spot, so (a) needs (c). A token with no feed has no composition to compute (report 05 M-01), so the Mandate should reject such tokens.
- **(b)** is a tolerance, and it leaks inside it, as the table shows.
  - Liveness: a legitimately deviated pool stops unwinds, mints and reports. The live 0.3% pools sat 0.26% (Robinhood) and 1.19% (Arbitrum) off the oracle.
  - Griefing: a band lets whoever controls ordering stop another holder's unwind.
  - With the spot-based 5% floor kept, the in-band leak can reach the band plus up to 5% of the WETH sold. In the listed pool the pool's own liquidity filled first, so the measured leak stayed at 1.74%.
- **(c)** leaks up to its bound, times the WETH share of each unwind.
  - It does not touch the Share Price: with (c) alone Alice still paid 248 on Bruno's partial claim.
  - It costs liveness in thin pools: the vault's own sale broke even today's spot-based floor from −5.2%, so a tighter floor makes more large claims partial.
  - When the floor reverts, the whole unwind rolls back (report 04 L-03), unless it is combined with (b)'s "stop, keep what was unwound".
- **(d)** needs wide per-token tolerances. A ±5% range's USDC leg moves about 20% per 1% of price, so a 1% gap between pool and oracle already shifts it by 20%. It misses one-sided positions, and it does not cover the swap.

Numbers that set a band:
- The Arbitrum ETH / USD feed moves on a 0.05% deviation: each of the 239 round-to-round changes in the sample was at least 0.0503%.
- In 6.24 h it updated every 29 to 810 s (median 60 s), with per-round moves of median 0.080%, p90 0.161% and maximum 0.487%. The heartbeat is not observable in that sample; Chainlink publishes 24 h.
- The listed pools charge 0.05%, and arbitrage kept them within 0.05% of the feed.

A band of about 1% for 0.05% pools, and about 1.5% for 0.3% pools, keeps normal operation live. With (c) at the same bound, it caps the in-band leak at roughly half the band times the unwound amount, because about half of a ±range exit is WETH.

## Findings

### [L-01] The payout fallback value (`lastHubValue`) is stored from whatever spot composition the last deposit or payout read
- Status: CONFIRMED (fork PoC passes; the failure of the hub read is mocked, see below)
- Where:
  - `src/core/CoreVaultLogic.sol:89-103` (`recordValuation`): line 98 stores the hub value of every successful deposit, request or claim.
  - `:219-232` (`_hubValue`): a PAYOUT read falls back to that value.
  - `:235-248` (`_positionsPrincipal`): the hub value is the V4 principal at the `slot0` composition.
  - Written from `src/core/CoreVault.sol:69`, `:105` and `:185`; moved by `src/core/CoreVaultTransit.sol:32` and `:47-48`.
- Rule: DEC-021 and DEC-056 (payout liveness, the fallback's purpose), DEC-067 (guarded price), the OQ-10 row (`docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md:54`), ARCHITECTURE §4.6 "Payout liveness".
- What: payout liveness keeps "the last successfully computed value" for claims whose hub read fails. That value is a spot-composition read, so a deposit made while the pool is pushed stores a pushed value. The docs say that "market moves since the last read are not seen", but not that the last read can itself be manipulated.
  - The deposit can be 2 USDC and can run inside one PoolManager unlock, with no capital beyond the fee.
  - Every later payout that falls back is priced with the pushed value, until a successful deposit or payout overwrites it.
- Scenario (fork, block 510408366):
  1. Alice deposits 250,000 and Bruno 50,000. The manager keeps 100,000 of Free Idle, opens a ±50% V4 position worth 100,000 and parks the rest in Aave. Bruno requests an Instant 40,000.
  2. A stranger, inside one unlock, pushes the pool under the range, deposits 2 USDC (1 share), and pushes it back.
  3. `lastHubValue` is now 224,532.12, while the live hub value is 199,133.58 (+25,398.54).
  4. The hub read fails for Bruno's claim (mocked `buildReport` revert). The claim emits `HubValuationFallback(224,532.12)` and burns 36,883 shares; with the read working it burns 40,015. Bruno keeps 3,132 more shares, and their value comes from the other holders.
- PoC: `test/review/integration-price/FallbackPoisonFork.t.sol`, `test_lastHubValueRecordsAPushedValuation`. Command as above. Result: 1 passed.
- Precondition and severity: Low. The hub read must fail.
  - Report 02 found no way for a stranger to cause that at normal position counts.
  - A claimant can force it by gas starvation only when the manager's positions make `buildReport` exceed about 9.9M gas (report 02 L-05), which is manager territory.
  - It matters most if the fix leaves claims priced at spot (option (b) alone).
- Fix: option (a) removes it, because the stored value is then at the oracle composition. Alternatively, do not refresh `lastHubValue` from a valuation whose pool deviates from the oracle beyond the band.
- Known?:
  - Report 02 C-01 covers the spot read, and 02 L-05 covers forcing the fallback.
  - Neither, nor OPEN-QUESTIONS or the REVIEW-LOG, says that the fallback inherits a manipulated read. Ground (b).

### [I-01] Every WETH/USDC Uniswap V4 pool a fund can list is thin, so moving it costs from cents to about 180 USDC
- Status: measured (fork probe and `cast`)
- Where:
  - `src/adapters/UniswapV4Adapter.sol:231`: native-currency pools are rejected.
  - `docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md:12`: there is no V3 adapter.
  - `docs/INTEGRATIONS.md:28`, `:49-50`: the listed pools.
- Rule: DEC-030 (closed pool list), DEC-067 and DEC-069 (the unwind sells in these pools), QA3.
- What: Deep WETH/USDC liquidity on Arbitrum sits in the V4 native-ETH pool (in-range L 6.43e17) and in the V3 pool (L 3.84e18). The adapter can use neither.
  - The Arbitrum V4 WETH/USDC pools are the listed 0.05% one (the whole downside is 36.8 WETH; 118 USDC to crush and restore) and an empty 0.3% one, 1.19% off the market.
  - On Robinhood, the listed pools hold 56.5 WETH (182 USDG round trip) and 5.6 WETH (96 USDG).
  - So every price PoC costs less than a few hundred dollars.
  - The unwind's own sale also breaks its 5% floor from a pushed price for 13.9 WETH. A large legitimate claim, after the fund's own liquidity leaves, would do the same; that case is reasoned from the probe, not run. The claim then becomes partial.
- Fix: disclose it in the Mandate documentation. If deeper markets are wanted, support native-ETH V4 pools (the adapter wraps and unwraps) or a V3 adapter. Neither removes the need for the price fix.
- Known?: no. The rejection of native pools is documented (`UniswapV4Adapter.sol` NatSpec); its effect on manipulation cost is not.

### [I-02] Mints revert whenever the ETH / USD feed has not moved 0.05% in the last hour
- Status: measured (feed history), reasoned
- Where: `script/FactoryDeployment.sol:70` and `:163-164` (a 1 h `maxPriceAge` for hub and Robinhood WETH); `src/core/CoreVaultLogic.sol:340-344` (`StalePrice` on a mint).
- Rule: OQ-10 (mints revert on a stale price), DEC-061.
- What: the feed updates on a 0.05% deviation (each of the 239 round-to-round changes sampled was at least 0.0503%), with a heartbeat that Chainlink publishes as 24 h. So a quiet hour ages the answer past the scripts' 1 h bound, and every deposit reverts until ETH moves 0.05%.
  - In the 6.24 h sample the longest gap was 810 s, so this is rare today; quiet weekends are when it would bite.
  - The project's own e2e scenario re-posts the answer after each warp to get past it (`test/fork/e2e/EndToEndBase.sol:159-169`).
- Fix: set `maxPriceAge` to the heartbeat plus a margin, and rely on a deviation band against the pool (option (b)) for freshness against the market. Or document that deposits pause in quiet markets.
- Known?: OQ-10 accepts mint reverts on stale prices; the mismatch between the 1 h bound and the feed's heartbeat is not discussed.

### [I-03] No test runs the automatic unwind at a moved price without the claimant's oracle hint
- Status: code read
- Where: `test/fork/e2e/EndToEnd.t.sol:626-629` and `:714-732`: the only fork test of the unwind passes a Chainlink-floored hint.
- What: that hint is option (c) applied by the claimant, and my third-party run shows it stops the deep push. Hints are optional, and the attacker passes `""`.
  - With report 04 I-05 (the unit mocks equate spot with the swap rate) and report 02 I-08 (no Core Vault test values a real V4 position), no test in the suite can see C-01.
- Fix: add fork tests with empty hints at a moved price; the PoCs here can serve.
- Known?: report 04 I-05 covers the unit tests only.

## Checked and found correct
1. **Report 04's claim that V4 flash accounting cannot wrap the unwind holds.** Inside the attacker's own unlock the PositionManager's `unlock` reverts `AlreadyUnlocked`, the Core Vault catches it (`UnwindForPayoutFailed`), and the position survives. The deep push needs outside capital, about 55 WETH here, which a flash loan provides.
2. **Report 02's zero-capital claim holds for an Idle-paid claim and for a spoke `report()`.** Neither unlocks the PoolManager, so both run between a swap and its reversal inside one unlock. The net cost is the round-trip fee.
3. **The report lifetime (DEC-099 and the Q57 reading) works as documented.** Mints revert `StaleSpokeReport` one second past 1,588 s; payouts keep the last accepted report; a higher sequence displaces it.
4. **Same-transaction deposits cannot trigger the unwind.** A deposit adds to Free Idle one for one (`CoreVault.sol:81`), so the attacker's stake must have been allocated before.
5. **The unwind does not depend on Aave.** When the crushed V4 step returns about 0, the Aave step pays the rest, so the attacker's claim completes. Without it, `unwindForPayout` returns what it holds (`SpokeVault.sol:541-545`), and the fund loses the position all the same.
6. **The zero-value skip saves nothing in practice.** A position whose spot value rounds to 0 is skipped (`SpokeVault.sol:858`), but at 1/1,000,000 of the price 38 WETH are still worth 0.10 USDC.
7. **Report 06's checked item 11 (the 5% floor holds for sales up to about 20 WETH) is consistent with this review.** It starts from the fair price; from a price pushed to −5.2%, a 13.9 WETH sale broke the floor in the same pool.
8. **Hub Operating Cash on factory funds starts at 0/0, so a claim tops up nothing.** Only the Instant Payout Fee enters it. Report 02 H-01, an unbounded manager setter, stands unchanged.
9. **Income realized during an unwind goes to the hub vault's collected bucket, not to the proceeds (DEC-092).** It is the fee the attacker's own round trip paid into the fund's range: 26 to 88 USDC per run.
10. **The oracle-composition math is right, which supports option (a).** On the honest report, my recomputation equals the adapter's principal to 577 base units (149,673.854170 against 149,673.853593), the pool-to-oracle gap. It stays exactly constant under every push.
11. **The deprecated adapter's exit verbs work, on both chains.** Close and collect ran in the tests (decrease is the same ungated path). Hub WETH income can still be forwarded and withdrawn in kind, since the forward never calls the adapter (code read); only principal WETH, and spoke WETH income, are stranded.
